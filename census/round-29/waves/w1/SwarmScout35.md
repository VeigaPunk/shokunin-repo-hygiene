6. Scope: the paper is about WEB DOCUMENTS (canonical token sequences), not byte-identical files. Evidence: "has led a proliferation of documents that are identical or almost identical" (12 words, PDF p.1, :21-22) and "The documents were retrieved from a walk of the World Wide Web performed by the Alta Vista search engine." (19 words, PDF p.2, :57-58). Section 2 first reduces a document with a parser to "a canonical sequence of tokens" and says documents differing only in formatting/punctuation/capitalisation are "reduces to the same sequence": quote "any two documents that differ only in formatting or other information that we chose to ignore" (16 words, PDF p.2, :74-75). So "identical" in the cluster result means identical after canonicalisation, not byte-equal; r=1 does not imply byte equality (it implies equal shingle sets, and for Option B even different multiplicities collapse). The paper therefore supports resemblance/containment as vocabulary for near-duplicate/syntactic similarity only; it is not a basis for a byte-equality verdict, which is what cmp(1) (the second staged entry) is for. Matches the preregistration wording `Broder resemblance/containment as vocabulary only`.
